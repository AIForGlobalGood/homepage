# AI For Global Good Foundation
## Organization Charter
> **Status:** Founding Draft v0.1 — October 2026
> This Charter is written to be jurisdiction-neutral. When the Foundation is formally registered, it will be adapted to the requirements of the chosen jurisdiction without changing its mission, principles, or asset lock. Sections marked `[JURISDICTION]` require legal review at that time.

---

## Preamble

We are living through a turning point. Artificial intelligence and other frontier technologies can widen the gap between those who have and those who have not, or they can become shared tools that lift all of humanity.

We, the founders of AI For Global Good, choose the second path. We believe the force of good thinking must become good doing: that knowledge, talent, and technology must be put to work, openly and responsibly, for the people and the planet that need them most, today and for the generations to come.

This Charter sets out who we are, what we stand for, and how we will govern ourselves so that our work stays faithful to that purpose as we grow.

---

## Article 1 — Name and Nature

1.1. The organization is named **AI For Global Good** (the "Foundation").

1.2. The Foundation is a non-profit, non-partisan, and independent organization serving the public good. It is not affiliated with any government, political party, religious body, or commercial enterprise.

1.3. The Foundation exists in perpetuity unless dissolved in accordance with Article 15.

1.4. Seat and legal form: `[JURISDICTION]` — to be defined at registration. Until then, the Foundation operates as a founding initiative governed by this Charter.

---

## Article 2 — Mission

The Foundation's mission is **to put artificial intelligence and frontier technologies to work for the common good of humanity and the planet, with priority given to those communities that are most underserved.**

---

## Article 3 — Vision

A world where the benefits of artificial intelligence are shared by everyone, where technology strengthens human dignity, equity, and the health of our planet, and where every community has the knowledge and tools to shape its own future.

---

## Article 4 — Guiding Principles

Everything the Foundation does is held to these principles:

1. **Human dignity first.** Technology serves people, never the reverse. We respect human rights, autonomy, and agency in everything we build and support.
2. **Equity and inclusion.** We prioritize those most affected by inequality, and we design *with* communities, not merely *for* them.
3. **Responsibility and safety.** We anticipate harm, evaluate risk, and choose not to act when the risks outweigh the benefits.
4. **Openness.** By default we share our knowledge, code, data, and results openly, unless doing so would put people's safety or privacy at risk.
5. **Transparency and accountability.** We are honest about our funding, decisions, successes, and failures.
6. **Scientific integrity.** Our work is grounded in evidence, rigorous methods, and independent review.
7. **Sustainability.** We consider long-term environmental, social, and economic impact, including the footprint of the technology itself.
8. **Independence.** No donor, partner, or member may direct the Foundation's work against its mission.
9. **Collaboration.** We achieve more together. We partner across disciplines, sectors, and borders.

---

## Article 5 — Purposes

In pursuit of its mission, the Foundation's purposes are to:

5.1. Develop, support, and deploy AI and technology solutions that address pressing global challenges, including but not limited to:
   - Global health and well-being
   - Climate, environment, and resilience
   - Education and digital literacy
   - Economic inclusion and poverty reduction
   - Humanitarian response and crisis preparedness
   - Responsible and ethical AI governance

5.2. Advance open knowledge by producing and sharing open-source software, open data, and open research.

5.3. Build capacity by training, mentoring, and empowering people and institutions, especially in under-resourced regions, to use and create AI responsibly.

5.4. Promote the safe, fair, and accountable use of AI through research, public education, and constructive engagement with policymakers and civil society.

5.5. Connect volunteers, experts, organizations, and funders around shared projects for the public good.

---

## Article 6 — Activities

To fulfil its purposes, the Foundation may:

6.1. Run, incubate, and fund projects, programs, and research.
6.2. Organize events, hackathons, fellowships, courses, and convenings.
6.3. Publish reports, toolkits, guidelines, and educational materials.
6.4. Form partnerships with academic, governmental, non-governmental, and private-sector organizations, provided such partnerships are consistent with this Charter.
6.5. Receive donations, grants, sponsorships, and in-kind contributions in accordance with Article 11.
6.6. Establish chapters, affiliates, and programs as provided in Article 13.
6.7. Undertake any other lawful activity in direct service of its mission.

---

## Article 7 — Responsible Technology Commitments

7.1. **Do no harm.** The Foundation will not develop, support, or knowingly enable technology intended for:
   - weapons or the targeting of people;
   - unlawful mass surveillance;
   - manipulation or disinformation;
   - discrimination or the suppression of human rights.

7.2. **Impact and risk review.** Every significant project must undergo an ethics and risk review before launch, and again at major milestones.

7.3. **Privacy and data stewardship.** Personal data is collected only when necessary, protected by design, and handled with informed consent. Data about communities belongs, in spirit, to those communities.

7.4. **Human oversight.** Systems that affect people's lives must keep meaningful human oversight and offer clear ways to seek redress.

7.5. **Fairness.** Projects must actively assess and mitigate bias across the populations they affect.

---

## Article 8 — Membership and Participation

8.1. The Foundation is open to individuals and organizations who share its mission and agree to uphold this Charter and its Code of Conduct.

8.2. Categories of participation:
   - **Founders:** those who established the Foundation and signed this Charter.
   - **Members:** individuals who formally join and contribute to its work.
   - **Volunteers and contributors:** anyone who contributes time, skills, code, or knowledge.
   - **Partners:** organizations that collaborate under a formal agreement.
   - **Supporters:** donors and sponsors.

8.3. Participation in the Foundation does not confer any ownership of its assets.

8.4. The Board may suspend or end the participation of anyone who seriously or repeatedly violates this Charter or the Code of Conduct, after giving them a fair opportunity to be heard.

---

## Article 9 — Governance

9.1. **Board of Trustees.** The Board is the Foundation's highest governing body, responsible for safeguarding its mission, approving strategy and budgets, and overseeing management.
   - It has between 3 and 11 Trustees.
   - Trustees serve terms of 3 years, renewable once in a row.
   - The Board should reflect a diversity of geography, gender, discipline, and lived experience.
   - Decisions require a simple majority of Trustees present, with a quorum of more than half the Board. Amendments to this Charter, admitting affiliates, and dissolution require the supermajorities set out in Articles 14 and 15.
   - Trustees serve without pay for their governance role. Reasonable expenses may be reimbursed.

9.2. **Executive Director.** The Board may appoint an Executive Director to lead day-to-day operations, staff, and programs, accountable to the Board.

9.3. **Ethics and Impact Committee.** A standing committee, with at least one member independent of the Board, reviews projects under Article 7, advises the Board, and may recommend pausing or ending any activity.

9.4. **Advisory Council.** The Board may appoint an Advisory Council of experts and community representatives to provide guidance. The Council has no binding authority.

9.5. **Founding period.** Until the first Board is constituted, the Founders act collectively as the Interim Board, with all its powers and duties under this Charter. The first Board must be constituted within 18 months of adoption of this Charter, or upon legal registration, whichever comes first.

---

## Article 10 — Conflicts of Interest and Integrity

10.1. Trustees, officers, staff, and committee members must disclose any personal, financial, or professional interest that conflicts, or could appear to conflict, with the interests of the Foundation.

10.2. Anyone with a conflict must abstain from the related discussion and decision.

10.3. The Foundation will maintain a written Conflict of Interest Policy and a Whistleblower Policy that protects those who report concerns in good faith.

---

## Article 11 — Funding, Assets, and Independence

11.1. **Non-distribution.** The Foundation's income and assets are used only to advance its mission. No part of them may be distributed to Founders, Trustees, members, or any private person, except as reasonable payment for services actually provided.

11.2. **Gift acceptance.** The Foundation accepts funding only when it is consistent with its mission and principles. It declines any contribution that would compromise its independence, reputation, or commitments under Article 7.

11.3. **Diversified funding.** The Foundation aims to keep diverse sources of funding. No single funder should account for a share of revenue that could compromise its independence; the Board will set and publish this limit.

11.4. **Financial stewardship.** The Foundation keeps proper accounts, adopts an annual budget, and submits its accounts to independent review or audit as soon as its size or the law requires.

---

## Article 12 — Transparency and Reporting

12.1. The Foundation publishes, at minimum once per year:
   - an Annual Report of activities and impact;
   - financial statements;
   - a list of major funders and partners, unless a donor requests anonymity and the gift raises no conflict;
   - a summary of its governance, including Board composition.

12.2. Unless doing so would compromise safety or privacy, project results, including negative results, are made public.

---

## Article 13 — Growth: Chapters, Affiliates, and Federation

The Foundation is founded with the ambition to grow from a founding initiative into a global movement.

13.1. **Chapters.** The Board may recognize local or regional Chapters that adopt this Charter, operate under the Foundation's name, and report to it.

13.2. **Affiliates.** Independent organizations that share the mission may become Affiliates by agreement, keeping their own legal identity while committing to this Charter's principles.

13.3. **Federation.** As the network grows, the Board may propose turning the Foundation into an international federation of national entities, with a global secretariat. Any such change must preserve Articles 2, 4, 7, and 11.

13.4. **Use of name.** The name "AI For Global Good" and its marks may be used only with authorization from the Foundation.

13.5. **Indicative path of growth** (non-binding):
   - **Stage I: Founding Initiative.** Charter adopted, founding circle formed, first projects and public site launched.
   - **Stage II: Registered Foundation.** Legal registration, first Board constituted, policies adopted, first funded programs.
   - **Stage III: Global Network.** Chapters and Affiliates in multiple regions, fellowships, multi-year partnerships.
   - **Stage IV: International Federation.** Federated structure, global governance assembly, permanent endowment.

---

## Article 14 — Amendments

14.1. This Charter may be amended by a two-thirds (2/3) vote of the full Board, after the proposed amendment has been circulated to members at least 30 days in advance for comment.

14.2. **Entrenched provisions.** Articles 2 (Mission), 4 (Guiding Principles), 7 (Responsible Technology Commitments), 11.1 (Non-distribution), and 15 (Dissolution) may be amended only by a unanimous vote of the full Board, and never in a way that permits private benefit or abandons the public-good mission.

---

## Article 15 — Dissolution

15.1. The Foundation may be dissolved by a three-quarters (3/4) vote of the full Board, if continuing its mission is no longer possible.

15.2. On dissolution, after paying all its liabilities, any remaining assets must be transferred to one or more non-profit organizations with a similar mission, as chosen by the Board. They may never be distributed to any private person. `[JURISDICTION]`

---

## Article 16 — Founding Documents and Policies

This Charter is the Foundation's supreme governing document. The Board will adopt and publish supporting policies, including:

- Code of Conduct
- Conflict of Interest Policy
- Whistleblower Policy
- Gift Acceptance Policy
- Responsible AI and Project Review Policy
- Privacy and Data Policy
- Open Source and Intellectual Property Policy

If any policy conflicts with this Charter, the Charter prevails.
