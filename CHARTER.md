# Building AI For Global Good
## Organization Charter
> **Status:** Founding Draft v0.3 — October 2026
> This Charter is written to be jurisdiction-neutral. When the Organization is formally registered, it will be adapted to the requirements of the chosen jurisdiction without changing its mission, principles, or asset lock. Sections marked `[JURISDICTION]` require legal review at that time.

---

## Preamble

We are living through a turning point. Artificial intelligence and the digital technologies around it can widen the gap between those who have and those who have not, or they can become shared tools that lift all of humanity.

We, the founders of Building AI For Global Good, choose the second path. We believe the force of good thinking must become good doing: that knowledge, talent, and technology must be put to work, openly and responsibly, for the people and the planet that need them most, today and for the generations to come.

This Charter sets out who we are, what we stand for, and how we will govern ourselves so that our work stays faithful to that purpose as we grow.

---

## Article 1 — Name and Nature

1.1. The organization is named **Building AI For Global Good** (the "Organization"). It may be referred to in short form as "AI For Global Good" where context makes the reference unambiguous.

1.2. The Organization is a non-profit, independent organization serving the public good. It is not controlled by any government, political party, religious body, or commercial enterprise. The Organization is **non-partisan** — it does not endorse or oppose political parties or candidates — but it is not apolitical: it engages with public policy where that serves its mission.

1.3. The Organization is established for an indefinite term and continues until dissolved in accordance with Article 15.

1.4. Seat and legal form: `[JURISDICTION]` — to be defined at registration. Until then, the Organization operates as a founding initiative governed by this Charter.

1.5. **Name, brand, and independence from other organizations.** The public brand of the Organization is *Building AI For Global Good*, with the short-form *AI For Global Good* and the primary domain `aiforglobalgood.org`. The Organization is independent and is **not affiliated with**, endorsed by, or operated in partnership with:
   - the *AI for Good Foundation* (ai4good.org);
   - the *AI for Good* platform of the International Telecommunication Union (aiforgood.itu.int) or any United Nations body;
   - the *Erasmus AI for Global Good Initiative* operated by ESAA;
   - any other similarly named organization.

   The legal name registered in the chosen jurisdiction may differ from the public brand; where the two differ, the Organization will operate under a *doing-business-as* (DBA) or equivalent registration that makes the relationship clear.

## Article 2 — Mission

The Organization's mission is **to put artificial intelligence, and the digital technologies that enable or complement it (together, "AI and related technologies"), to work for the common good of humanity and the planet, with priority given to those communities that are most underserved.**

---

## Article 3 — Vision

A world where the benefits of artificial intelligence are shared by everyone, where technology strengthens human dignity, equity, and the health of our planet, and where every community has the knowledge and tools to shape its own future.

---

## Article 4 — Guiding Principles

Everything the Organization does is held to these principles:

1. **Human dignity first.** Technology serves people, never the reverse. We respect human rights, autonomy, and agency in everything we build and support.
2. **Equity and inclusion.** We prioritize those most affected by inequality, and we design *with* communities, not merely *for* them.
3. **Responsibility and safety.** We anticipate harm, evaluate risk, and choose not to act when the risks outweigh the benefits.
4. **Openness.** By default we share our knowledge, code, data, and results openly, unless doing so would put people's safety or privacy at risk. Software we release is licensed under OSI-approved open-source licenses; written content under permissive Creative Commons licenses; data under recognized open-data licenses where safe to do so.
5. **Transparency and accountability.** We are honest about our funding, decisions, successes, and failures.
6. **Scientific integrity.** Our work is grounded in evidence, rigorous methods, and independent review.
7. **Sustainability.** We consider long-term environmental, social, and economic impact, including the footprint of the technology itself.
8. **Independence.** No donor, partner, or member may direct the Organization's work against its mission.
9. **Collaboration.** We achieve more together. We partner across disciplines, sectors, and borders.

---

## Article 5 — Purposes

In pursuit of its mission, the Organization's purposes are to:

5.1. Develop, support, and deploy AI and technology solutions that address pressing global challenges, including but not limited to:
   - Global health and well-being
   - Climate, environment, and resilience
   - Education and digital literacy
   - Economic inclusion and poverty reduction
   - Humanitarian response and crisis preparedness
   - Responsible and ethical AI governance

5.2. Advance open knowledge by producing and sharing open-source software, open data, and open research.

5.3. Build capacity by training, mentoring, and empowering people and institutions, especially in under-resourced regions, to use and create AI responsibly.

5.4. Promote the safe, fair, and accountable use of AI through research, public education, and constructive engagement with policymakers and civil society.

5.5. Connect volunteers, experts, organizations, and funders around shared projects for the public good.

5.6. Incubate projects that may be shared openly with partners in low- and middle-income settings, or, where appropriate, spun out into independent mission-aligned entities in accordance with Article 18.

## Article 6 — Activities

To fulfil its purposes, the Organization may:

6.1. Run, incubate, and fund projects, programs, and research.
6.2. Organize events, hackathons, fellowships, courses, and convenings.
6.3. Publish reports, toolkits, guidelines, and educational materials.
6.4. Form partnerships with academic, governmental, non-governmental, and private-sector organizations, provided such partnerships are consistent with this Charter.
6.5. Receive donations, grants, sponsorships, and in-kind contributions in accordance with Article 11.
6.6. Establish chapters, affiliates, and programs as provided in Article 13.
6.7. License intellectual property to, and under limited conditions hold interests in, independent spin-out entities, as provided in Article 18.
6.8. Undertake any other lawful activity in direct service of its mission.

## Article 7 — Responsible Technology Commitments

7.1. **Do no harm.** The Organization will not develop, support, or knowingly enable technology intended for, or whose foreseeable use is:
   - weapons systems, or the targeting of persons for physical or digital harm;
   - mass surveillance of populations, measured against international human rights standards (including Article 17 of the International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights), regardless of whether such surveillance is lawful in a given jurisdiction;
   - deception, manipulation, or disinformation that undermines informed consent, personal autonomy, or democratic processes;
   - discrimination against, or suppression of the human rights of, any individual or group.

7.2. **Impact and risk review.** Every significant project must undergo an ethics and risk review before launch, and again at major milestones.

7.3. **Privacy and data stewardship.** Personal data is collected only when necessary, protected by design, and handled with informed consent. Data about communities belongs, in spirit, to those communities.

7.4. **Human oversight.** Systems that affect people's lives must keep meaningful human oversight and offer clear ways to seek redress.

7.5. **Fairness.** Projects must actively assess and mitigate bias across the populations they affect, using an explicit, documented fairness standard appropriate to the context.

7.6. **Alignment with recognized frameworks.** The Organization's project-review methodology takes as its baseline the OECD AI Principles, the UNESCO Recommendation on the Ethics of Artificial Intelligence, and the NIST AI Risk Management Framework, together with any binding law of the jurisdictions in which it operates. Where these conflict, the stricter standard applies.

7.7. **AI-specific commitments.** The Organization adopts, publishes, and maintains standards covering, at minimum:
   - **Training-data provenance and consent** — data used to train or fine-tune models must have a lawful and ethical basis, with respect for the rights of data subjects and creators;
   - **Labeler and moderator welfare** — people performing data labeling, annotation, or content moderation under the Organization's auspices or in its direct supply chain are paid at least a local living wage and work under safe conditions, with access to mental-health support where exposure warrants it;
   - **Model release** — for each released model, the Organization publishes a release-decision record covering capability, foreseeable misuse, and the chosen access tier (open weights, gated access, or closed);
   - **Compute footprint** — the Organization measures and reports the energy and greenhouse-gas footprint of its training and inference workloads, and prefers low-carbon compute where feasible;
   - **Pre-deployment evaluation** — systems that affect people's lives are evaluated and red-teamed before deployment and at material updates, and the results of those evaluations are published to the extent compatible with Article 7.3.

7.8. **Binding on spin-outs.** The commitments of this Article bind, by contract, any entity that is spun out of the Organization under Article 18, for as long as that entity uses intellectual property originating from the Organization or carries the Organization's endorsement.

## Article 8 — Membership and Participation

8.1. The Organization is open to individuals and organizations who share its mission and agree to uphold this Charter and its Code of Conduct.

8.2. Categories of participation:
   - **Founders:** those who established the Organization and signed this Charter.
   - **Members:** individuals who formally join and contribute to its work.
   - **Volunteers and contributors:** anyone who contributes time, skills, code, or knowledge.
   - **Partners:** organizations that collaborate under a formal agreement.
   - **Supporters:** donors and sponsors.

8.3. Participation in the Organization does not confer any ownership of its assets.

8.4. The Board may suspend or end the participation of anyone who seriously or repeatedly violates this Charter or the Code of Conduct, after giving them a fair opportunity to be heard.

---

## Article 9 — Governance

9.1. **Board of Trustees.** The Board is the Organization's highest governing body, responsible for safeguarding its mission, approving strategy and budgets, and overseeing management.
   - It has between 3 and 11 Trustees.
   - Trustees serve terms of 3 years. A Trustee may serve a maximum of two consecutive terms, and is then eligible to serve again only after a gap of at least one full term.
   - The Board should reflect a diversity of geography, gender, discipline, and lived experience.
   - Decisions require a simple majority of Trustees present and voting, with a quorum of more than half the Trustees then in office. Amendments to this Charter, admitting affiliates, and dissolution require the supermajorities set out in Articles 14 and 15. Where this Charter refers to a proportion of the **full Board**, that proportion is calculated against the number of Trustee seats then authorized by the Board, not the number currently filled.
   - Trustees serve without pay for their governance role. Reasonable expenses may be reimbursed.

9.2. **Executive Director.** The Board may appoint an Executive Director to lead day-to-day operations, staff, and programs, accountable to the Board. The Executive Director may not simultaneously serve as a Trustee, and the Executive Director's performance is reviewed by the Board at least annually.

9.3. **Ethics and Impact Committee.** A standing committee, a majority of whose members are independent of the Board and of Organization management, reviews projects under Article 7, advises the Board, and may recommend pausing or ending any activity. The Committee reports to the Board but may also publish summaries of its findings directly, subject to confidentiality and safety constraints.

9.4. **Advisory Council.** The Board may appoint an Advisory Council of experts and community representatives to provide guidance. The Council has no binding authority.

9.5. **Founding period.** Until the first Board is constituted, the Founders act collectively as the Interim Board, with all its powers and duties under this Charter. The first Board must be constituted within 18 months of adoption of this Charter, or upon legal registration, whichever comes first. **At least half of the first Board must be independent of the Founders** — that is, not a Founder, nor an employee, close relative, or business partner of a Founder. Obligations and liabilities incurred by the Interim Board during the founding period are assumed by the Organization upon its legal registration, to the extent permitted by the law of the chosen jurisdiction; until then, Founders act jointly in trust for the mission set out in this Charter.

## Article 10 — Conflicts of Interest and Integrity

10.1. Trustees, officers, staff, and committee members must disclose any personal, financial, or professional interest that conflicts, or could appear to conflict, with the interests of the Organization. This includes any direct or indirect financial interest in a spin-out entity under Article 18.

10.2. Anyone with a conflict must abstain from the related discussion and decision.

10.3. The Organization will maintain a written Conflict of Interest Policy and a Whistleblower Policy that protects those who report concerns in good faith.

---

## Article 11 — Funding, Assets, and Independence

11.1. **Non-distribution.** The Organization's income and assets are used only to advance its mission. No part of them may be distributed to Founders, Trustees, members, or any private person, except as:
   (a) reasonable payment for services actually provided to the Organization; or
   (b) proceeds flowing *to* the Organization from spin-out entities under Article 18, which are mission assets of the Organization and remain subject to this non-distribution rule.

   Nothing in this Charter authorizes the distribution of the Organization's assets to private persons for private benefit; spin-outs under Article 18 are permitted *because* they channel value back to the mission, not away from it.

11.2. **Gift acceptance.** The Organization accepts funding only when it is consistent with its mission and principles. It declines any contribution that would compromise its independence, reputation, or commitments under Article 7.

11.3. **Diversified funding.** The Organization aims to keep diverse sources of funding. As a default, no single funder, together with its affiliates, should account for more than **one-third (33%) of the Organization's revenue in any rolling three-year period**. The Board may tighten this cap by resolution and must publish the cap in force; it may loosen the cap only through the amendment procedure in Article 14.1, and never above a level that would compromise the Organization's independence.

11.4. **Financial stewardship.** The Organization keeps proper accounts, adopts an annual budget, and submits its accounts to independent review or audit as soon as its size or the law requires.

---

## Article 12 — Transparency and Reporting

12.1. The Organization publishes, at minimum once per year:
   - an Annual Report of activities and impact;
   - financial statements;
   - a list of major funders and partners, unless a donor requests anonymity and the gift raises no conflict;
   - a summary of its governance, including Board composition;
   - a register of active spin-outs under Article 18, including the Organization's interest in each.

12.2. Unless doing so would compromise safety or privacy, project results, including negative results, are made public.

---

## Article 13 — Growth: Chapters, Affiliates, and Federation

The Organization is founded with the ambition to grow from a founding initiative into a global movement.

13.1. **Chapters.** The Board may recognize local or regional Chapters that adopt this Charter, operate under the Organization's name, and report to it.

13.2. **Affiliates.** Independent organizations that share the mission may become Affiliates by agreement, keeping their own legal identity while committing to this Charter's principles.

13.3. **Federation.** As the network grows, the Board may propose turning the Organization into an international federation of national entities, with a global secretariat. Any such change must preserve Articles 2, 4, 7, and 11.

13.4. **Use of name.** The names "Building AI For Global Good" and "AI For Global Good," together with any associated marks and domains, may be used only with the authorization of the Organization. Chapters, Affiliates, and spin-outs under Article 18 use these names only under written agreement that preserves the Organization's control over the brand.

13.5. **Indicative path of growth** (non-binding):
   - **Stage I: Founding Initiative.** Charter adopted, founding circle formed, first projects and public site launched.
   - **Stage II: Registered Organization.** Legal registration, first Board constituted, policies adopted, first funded programs.
   - **Stage III: Global Network.** Chapters and Affiliates in multiple regions, fellowships, multi-year partnerships, first spin-outs.
   - **Stage IV: International Federation.** Federated structure, global governance assembly, permanent endowment.

## Article 14 — Amendments

14.1. This Charter may be amended by a two-thirds (2/3) vote of the full Board, after the proposed amendment has been circulated to all participation categories listed in Article 8.2 — Founders, Members, Volunteers and contributors, Partners, and Supporters — at least 30 days in advance for comment.

14.2. **Entrenched provisions.** Articles 2 (Mission), 4 (Guiding Principles), 7 (Responsible Technology Commitments), 11.1 (Non-distribution), 15 (Dissolution), and 18.1–18.3 (Spin-out mission lock) may be amended only by a four-fifths (4/5) vote of the full Board, and never in a way that permits private benefit or abandons the public-good mission.

---

## Article 15 — Dissolution

15.1. The Organization may be dissolved by a three-quarters (3/4) vote of the full Board, if continuing its mission is no longer possible.

15.2. On dissolution, after paying all its liabilities, any remaining assets — including any interests held in spin-out entities under Article 18 — must be transferred to one or more non-profit organizations with a similar mission, as chosen by the Board. They may never be distributed to any private person. `[JURISDICTION]`

17.1. **Fiscal year.** The Organization's fiscal year is the calendar year, unless the Board resolves otherwise to align with the requirements of the chosen jurisdiction.

17.2. **Working language.** The official working language of this Charter and of the Board is English. Translations may be published, but in case of conflict the English text prevails.

17.3. **Indemnification.** The Organization indemnifies Trustees, officers, committee members, staff, and volunteers against liabilities reasonably incurred in good-faith performance of their duties for the Organization, to the fullest extent permitted by `[JURISDICTION]` law. Indemnification does not extend to acts of fraud, willful misconduct, or knowing violation of this Charter.

## Article 18 — Projects, Intellectual Property, and Spin-outs

The Organization is a builder of things. Some projects remain inside the Organization and are shared openly; others grow to a scale or purpose where they are best carried forward by an independent entity. This Article governs that lifecycle so that mission comes first, no matter where a project ends up.

18.1. **Default IP ownership.** All intellectual property created by Trustees, officers, staff, contractors, or formally engaged contributors in the course of work for the Organization is owned by the Organization, unless a prior written agreement provides otherwise. Volunteer contributions to the Organization's open-source projects are licensed under the project's declared open-source license and need not be assigned.

18.2. **Default release terms.** Unless the Board resolves otherwise for a specific project with written reasons:
   - software is released under an OSI-approved open-source license;
   - content under a permissive Creative Commons license;
   - data under a recognized open-data license, subject to Article 7.3 (privacy and community data).

18.3. **Spin-outs — permitted and bounded.** The Organization may create or support an independent entity ("Spin-out") to carry a project to scale, where the Board, on the recommendation of the Ethics and Impact Committee, finds that:
   (a) a Spin-out is the most effective path to deliver the project's mission benefit;
   (b) a legally binding **mission-lock covenant** is in place, requiring the Spin-out to uphold Article 7 of this Charter for as long as it uses IP originating from the Organization or is publicly associated with the Organization's name;
   (c) the IP is **licensed** to the Spin-out, not transferred outright, except where the Board, by a two-thirds vote and in writing, finds that an outright transfer better serves the mission;
   (d) any **equity, membership interests, or revenue-share rights** granted to the Organization are held as mission assets of the Organization under Article 11.1 and are not distributable to Founders, Trustees, members, or staff;
   (e) a **change-of-control clause** is in place, requiring Organization approval before any sale, merger, or ownership change of the Spin-out, and permitting the Organization to revoke the license and the use of its name if the mission-lock is breached;
   (f) the arrangement is **published** in the register required by Article 12.1.

18.4. **Private interests of Trustees, officers, and staff.** No Founder, Trustee, officer, employee, or close relative or business partner of the foregoing may hold personal equity, options, or similar economic interest in a Spin-out at the time of its creation, except on terms disclosed to and approved by the Board under Article 10, with the interested person recused. Later participation is permitted only through the Board-approved Projects, IP, and Spin-out Policy and must be publicly disclosed.

18.5. **Shared projects without spin-out.** Projects that remain inside the Organization and are shared with partners (including in low- and middle-income settings) do so under the Organization's open licenses or under bespoke partnership agreements consistent with this Charter.

18.6. **Policy.** The Board adopts and publishes a Projects, IP, and Spin-out Policy implementing this Article, including standard mission-lock, change-of-control, and license terms.
